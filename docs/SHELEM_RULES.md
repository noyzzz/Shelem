# Shelem Rules — Working Draft

This document defines the rules the online game will enforce. It is a working
draft and should be corrected to match the version played by our group.

## Players and teams

- Four players sit around the table.
- Players sitting opposite each other are partners.
- Play proceeds clockwise.

## Deck and card values

Shelem uses a standard 52-card deck with no jokers.

Cards rank from highest to lowest:

`A, K, Q, J, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2`

Scoring cards and tricks:

- Each Ace: 10 points
- Each 10: 10 points
- Each 5: 5 points
- Each completed trick: 5 points

There are 165 available points in every hand.

## Deal

- Each player receives 12 cards.
- Four cards are placed face down in the middle as the ground (`zamin`).
- The deal rotates after each hand.

## Bidding

- Bidding starts with the player after the dealer.
- The first bidder cannot pass and opens the bidding at 100.
- A player may bid or pass.
- The minimum bid is 100.
- Bids increase in increments of 5, up to 165.
- A player who passes cannot bid again during that hand.
- Bidding ends when only one active bidder remains.
- Because the first player must open at 100, a hand is never redealt because
  everybody passed.

## Ground and trump

- After bidding ends, the four ground cards are revealed to every player.
- The reveal remains visible briefly before play continues.
- The winning bidder takes the four ground cards into their hand.
- The bidder discards any four cards face down.
- The discarded cards count toward the bidder's team's captured points.
- Trump is not declared during the ground phase.

## Playing tricks

- The bidder leads the first trick.
- The suit of the bidder's opening card becomes trump for the hand.
- Players must follow the suit that was led when possible.
- A player who cannot follow suit may play any card, including a trump.
- The highest trump wins the trick if one or more trumps were played.
- Otherwise, the highest card of the suit led wins.
- The winner of a trick leads the next trick.
- After the fourth card is played, the completed trick remains visible briefly
  so every player can review it before the cards are cleared.

## Hand scoring

At the end of the hand, each team first counts:

- points on captured Aces, 10s, and 5s;
- five points for each captured trick; and
- any points contained in the bidder's four discarded cards; and
- five points for the bidder's four-card discard pile as the thirteenth
  scoring trick.

The twelve played tricks are worth 60 points, the discard pile is worth 5,
and the scoring cards are worth 100, for a total of 165 points.

The counted points are then applied to the match score:

- If the bidding team meets or exceeds its bid, it adds all the points it
  captured, not merely the amount it bid.
- If the bidding team misses its bid, it subtracts exactly the amount it bid.
- The defending team adds the points it captured unless it was Shelemed.
- If the bidding team captures all 165 points, it adds 165 and the defending
  team subtracts 165, regardless of the bid.

### Examples

- A team bids 140 and captures 145 points: it adds 145.
- A team bids 140 and captures exactly 140 points: it adds 140.
- A team bids 140 and captures 135 points: it subtracts 140.
- A team bids 120 and captures all 165 points: it adds 165 and the defending
  team subtracts 165.

## Winning the match

- The target score is 1,000 points.
- There is no doubling of bids or scores.
- If both teams reach 1,000 in the same hand, the team that won the bid for
  that hand wins the match.

## Abandonment and disconnection

- A player who abandons the match loses.
- A disconnected player also loses.
- A disconnected player has 60 seconds to reconnect.
- If the player does not return during that grace period, their team forfeits
  the entire match.
